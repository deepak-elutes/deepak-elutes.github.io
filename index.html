RT 01:15 — About

About

Aa

I'm a PhD candidate in the Wang Lab at the University of Kansas, where I've worked since August 2022 toward an expected May 2027 graduation. My work sits at the intersection of environmental and biological analytical chemistry.

I develop LC-MS/MS and UPLC-MRM methods sensitive enough to quantify PFAS at sub-part-per-trillion levels in drinking water, to assess human exposure, and to measure small molecules and proteins inside biological tissue.

I also studied natural product chemistry — screening plant-derived compounds for enzyme-inhibitory bioactivity through in-silico docking and ADMET modeling.

RT 03:40 — Research

Research pillars

PFAS · SPE-UPLC-MS/MS

Ultra-trace PFAS detection

Fast-flow solid-phase extraction paired with UPLC-MS/MS to quantify PFAS in drinking water at sub-part-per-trillion detection limits — sensitive enough to see contamination most methods miss.

ZEOLITES · CYANOBACTERIA

PFAS remediation

Evaluating sorbents and cyanobacteria for pulling PFAS out of contaminated water, working toward regenerable, field-compatible treatment media.

FAST-FLOW SPE · PASSIVE SAMPLING

Field-deployable sample prep

A miniaturized and pocket-sized sample preparation device that brings EPA Priority 40 PFAS sample preparation out of the lab and into the field.

UPLC-MRM · TARGETED PROTEOMICS

Quantitative proteomics of membrane protein

Targeted proteomics in biological tissue via UPLC-MRM, to understand the disposition of contaminants and drugs in the animal and human body
